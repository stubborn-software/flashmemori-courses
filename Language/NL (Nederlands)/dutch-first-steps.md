# Before you start

Welcome! This course walks through the first weeks of a Dutch class. Every chapter follows the same
rhythm: a handful of **words**, a few **sentences** that use those words, and a short **grammar
pointer** where one is needed. Grammar you can practise gets its own table; grammar you just need to
read is a page like this one.

A few sounds worth knowing before the first word:

- **g** and **ch** are the same sound: a soft rasp from the back of the throat (*goed*, *acht*). Not the English g.
- **oe** sounds like the *oo* in *boot*: *goed*, *moeder*.
- **ee** and **oo** are long: *twee* rhymes with *day*, *brood* with *boat*.
- **ui** and **ij / ei** have no English equivalent; *huis* is roughly *house* said with pursed lips, *trein* is close to *train*.
- A **double consonant keeps the vowel short**: *man* (man) vs *maan* (moon). You will see this in plurals: *man → mannen*, *maan → manen*.
- **-tje / -je** at the end of a word is the diminutive: *huisje* (little house). Diminutives are always *het*-words.

When a word has two common forms they are written `hoi / hallo` and read aloud one after the other.
Tap the speaker on any row to hear the Dutch. For anything else, [Van Dale](https://www.vandale.nl/)
is the reference dictionary (the link opens outside the app).

# 1. Hallo!

In this chapter you greet people, say who you are and where you live, and count to twelve.

## 1.1 Greetings

Dutch greetings follow the time of day, and most of them are **one word**: *goede* + *morgen*
becomes *goedemorgen*. *Hoi* and *doei* are the informal pair you will hear all day among friends.

### Grammar: the verb zijn

*Zijn* (to be) is irregular, like in every language. Learn these forms now; everything else in

**-t** (*hij woont*), and all plural forms are the infinitive (*wij wonen*).

# 2. Mensen

People: your family, who belongs to whom, and the two little words that every Dutch noun comes
with.

## 2.1 De, het, een

Dutch nouns come in two kinds. **De**-words take *de* (about two thirds of all nouns) and
**het**-words take *het*. There is no reliable rule, so learn the article **with** the noun:
*de man*, *het kind*. The tables in this course give the article in its own column so you can
hide it or practise it.

A few shortcuts that do work:

- Every **plural** is a *de*-word: *het kind → de kinderen*.
- Every **diminutive** (*-je*, *-tje*, *-pje*) is a *het*-word: *het meisje*, *het huisje*.
- People and professions are almost always *de*: *de vader*, *de lerares*.
- The indefinite article is **een** for both kinds: *een man*, *een kind*.

The article also changes **this** and **that**: *deze man / dit kind*, *die man / dat kind*.

### Grammar: plurals

Most plurals end in **-en**. Words ending in **-el, -er, -en, -je**, and most loanwords, take
**-s**; words ending in a long vowel take **'s** (*foto's*, *auto's*). Watch the spelling: a short
vowel keeps its single letter and **doubles the consonant** (*man → mannen*), a long vowel written
with two letters **loses one** (*maan → manen*, *boot → boten*). A few are simply irregular.

| ?         | !          | +                                                                              |
|-----------|------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| men       | mannen     | Short **a**: the **n** doubles.                                                |
| women     | vrouwen    |                                                                                |
| children  | kinderen   | Irregular: **-eren**. Same for *ei → eieren* (eggs).                           |
| girls     | meisjes    | **-je** words take **-s**.                                                     |
| cities    | steden     | Irregular: the vowel changes, *stad → steden*.                                 |
| houses    | huizen     | **s** becomes **z** between vowels: *huis → huizen*.                           |
| boats     | boten      | Long **oo** written once in *boten*, still pronounced long.                    |
| cars      | auto's     | Vowel ending: apostrophe + **s**.                                              |
| tables    | tafels     | **-el** words take **-s**.                                                     |
| brothers  | broers     |                                                                                |

# 3. Eten en drinken

## 3.1 In the café

Ordering is simple once you have two frames: **Mag ik … ?** (may I have …?) and

### Grammar: word order

The one rule: in a statement the **conjugated verb is always the second element**. Usually the
subject comes first (*Ik ga morgen naar Amsterdam*). Put anything else first for emphasis and the
subject jumps behind the verb (*Morgen ga ik naar Amsterdam*). Any second verb, an infinitive or a
participle, waits at the **end** of the sentence.

| ?                                  | !                              | +                                                                                       |
|------------------------------------|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I am going to Amsterdam tomorrow.  | Ik ga morgen naar Amsterdam.   | Subject, verb, the rest.                                                                |
| Tomorrow I am going to Amsterdam.  | Morgen ga ik naar Amsterdam.   | *Morgen* first, so **ga** stays second and **ik** moves behind it.                      |
| I want to buy a ticket.            | Ik wil een kaartje kopen.      | The infinitive **kopen** goes to the end.                                               |
| We can walk.                       | Wij kunnen lopen.              |                                                                                         |
| Today the shop is closed.          | Vandaag is de winkel gesloten. | Time word first, verb second, participle **gesloten** last.                             |
| I bought bread yesterday.          | Ik heb gisteren brood gekocht. | Perfect tense: **heb** second, **gekocht** at the end.                                  |

#### Separable verbs

Many verbs are a **prefix + verb** that split in the present tense: *opstaan* (to get up) becomes
*ik sta op*, with the prefix at the end. After a modal they stay in one piece: *ik moet opstaan*.

| ?                              | !                              | +                                                                       |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| to get up                      | opstaan                        |                                                                         |
| I get up at seven.             | Ik sta om zeven uur op.        | **op** goes to the end.                                                 |
| to get off (a bus, a train)    | uitstappen                     | Getting on is **instappen**.                                            |
| We get off here.               | Wij stappen hier uit.          |                                                                         |
| to arrive                      | aankomen                       |                                                                         |
| The train arrives at ten.      | De trein komt om tien uur aan. |                                                                         |
| I have to get up early.        | Ik moet vroeg opstaan.         | After **moet** the verb stays whole, at the end.                        |

## 4.2 What time is it?

Full hours are easy: *het is drie uur*. The trap is the **half hour**: Dutch counts towards the
**next** hour, so *half vier* is **3:30**, not 4:30. Quarters use *kwart over* / *kwart voor*, and
minutes near the half hour hang on it: *vijf voor half vier* is 3:25.

| ?                                | !                                     | +                                                                                  |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| What time is it?                 | Hoe laat is het?                      | Literally "how late is it?".                                                       |
| It is three o'clock.             | Het is drie uur.                      |                                                                                    |
| It is half past three.           | Het is half vier.                     | **Half of the way to four.** The classic mistake: it is not 4:30.                  |
| It is a quarter past three.      | Het is kwart over drie.               |                                                                                    |
| It is a quarter to four.         | Het is kwart voor vier.               |                                                                                    |
| It is ten past three.            | Het is tien over drie.                |                                                                                    |
| It is twenty-five past three.    | Het is vijf voor half vier.           | "Five before half four" = 3:25.                                                    |
| at seven o'clock                 | om zeven uur                          | **om** before a clock time.                                                        |
| in the morning                   | 's ochtends / 's morgens              | The **'s** is an old genitive: "of the morning". Same for the other parts of the day. |
| in the afternoon                 | 's middags                            |                                                                                    |
| in the evening                   | 's avonds                             |                                                                                    |
| at night                         | 's nachts                             |                                                                                    |
| today / tomorrow / yesterday     | vandaag / morgen / gisteren           |                                                                                    |
| Monday                           | maandag                               | Days are lowercase: *maandag, dinsdag, woensdag, donderdag, vrijdag, zaterdag, zondag*. |

# Appendix

## Hebben and gaan

Two more irregular verbs you cannot avoid: **hebben** (to have) and **gaan** (to go). *Gaan* is also
how Dutch says what you are **going to** do: *ik ga eten* (I'm going to eat).

| ?                        | !                  | +                                                                 |
|--------------------------|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I have                   | ik heb             |                                                                   |
| you have (informal)      | jij hebt           | Question: *heb je?*                                               |
| you have (formal)        | u heeft            | *u hebt* is also correct and a little less formal.                |
| he has                   | hij heeft          |                                                                   |
| we have                  | wij hebben         |                                                                   |
| I go                     | ik ga              |                                                                   |
| you go                   | jij gaat           | Question: *ga je?*                                                |
| he goes                  | hij gaat           |                                                                   |
| they go                  | zij gaan           |                                                                   |
| I am going to eat.       | Ik ga eten.        | **gaan** + infinitive = going to.                                 |

## Pronunciation cheat sheet

A page to come back to, not to memorise.

- **a** short as in *cat* (*man*); **aa** long, open (*maan*).
- **e** short as in *bed* (*bed*); **ee** like *day* without the glide (*twee*); unstressed **e** is a schwa (*de*, *lopen*).
- **i** short as in *bit* (*dit*); **ie** like *see* (*drie*).
- **o** short as in *not* (*bot*); **oo** like *boat* (*brood*).
- **u** short, rounded, between *put* and *pit* (*bus*); **uu** like French *u* (*uur*).
- **oe** like *boot* (*goed*). **eu** like French *deux* (*neus*).
- **ij / ei** between *day* and *die* (*trein*, *ijs*). **ui** the hardest one (*huis*). **ou / au** like *house* (*oud*, *blauw*).
- **g / ch** the throat rasp (*goed*, *acht*). **sch** is *s* + that rasp (*school*). Final **-sch** is just *s* (*Nederlands*).
- **w** is softer than English, close to *v*; **v** is close to *f* at the start of a word.
- **-en** at the end of a word: the **n** is usually silent (*lopen* sounds like *lope*).
