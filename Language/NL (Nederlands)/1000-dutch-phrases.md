# 2 – Introduction

Hoofdstuk 2 – Introductie

| ?                                                    | !                                                            | +                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Good morning                                         | Goedemorgen                                                  | **Goede** + **morgen**, written as one word. The Dutch **g** is a soft rasping sound from the back of the throat, not the English g.                                                   |
| Good afternoon                                       | Goedemiddag                                                  | **middag** is midday/afternoon; this greeting is used from about noon until 6 pm. Also one word.                                                                                       |
| Good night                                           | Goedenacht                                                   | Said when going to bed or leaving late at night. To greet someone in the evening use **goedenavond**.                                                                                  |
| Hello, how are you?                                  | Hallo, hoe gaat het?                                         | Literally "how goes it?". Dutch asks about wellbeing with impersonal **het gaat**; the standard answer is **het gaat goed**.                                                           |
| Good morning, my name is Alex.                       | Goedemorgen, mijn naam is Alex.                              | Correct, but Dutch speakers more often say **Ik heet Alex**, from **heten** (to be called).                                                                                            |
| What’s your name?                                    | Wat is je naam?                                              | **je** is the informal "your"; the formal one is **uw**. The everyday alternative is **Hoe heet je?**                                                                                  |
| Nice to meet you.                                    | Leuk je te ontmoeten.                                        | **te** + infinitive goes at the end, like English "to meet". A more formal option is **Aangenaam** (pleased to meet you).                                                              |
| I live in Ireland.                                   | Ik woon in Ierland.                                          | **wonen** means to live somewhere (reside); **leven** means to be alive. Don't mix them up.                                                                                            |
| I’m fine, how are you?                               | Het gaat goed, hoe gaat het met je?                          | Literally "it goes well, how goes it with you?". **Ik ben goed** is an anglicism; say **het gaat goed**.                                                                               |
| I’m on vacation, I came to know this wonderful city. | Ik ben op vakantie, ik heb deze prachtige stad leren kennen. | **leren kennen** = get to know. In the perfect tense a verb like **leren** stays an infinitive next to the other one, and both go to the end: **heb ... leren kennen**.                |
| I like music, culture, museums and nature.           | Ik hou van muziek, cultuur, musea en de natuur.              | **houden van** = to love/like a lot; **ik hou** is the spoken form of **ik houd**. **musea** is the plural of **museum**, and **natuur** takes an article here (**de natuur**).        |
| My wife’s name is Anne, she’s French.                | Mijn vrouw heet Anne, zij is Frans.                          | Dutch says "my wife is called" (**heet**). **vrouw** means both woman and wife; nationality words keep their capital: **Frans**.                                                       |
| I do not have children.                              | Ik heb geen kinderen.                                        | Use **geen** (not **niet**) to negate a noun that has no article or would take **een**. Think of it as "no children".                                                                  |
| I have two children, a boy and a girl.               | Ik heb twee kinderen, een jongen en een meisje.              | **kind → kinderen** is an irregular plural. **meisje** ends in the diminutive **-je**, so it is a **het**-word: **het meisje**.                                                        |
| Sorry, please, thank you.                            | Sorry, alsjeblieft, bedankt.                                 | **alsjeblieft** is informal (it contains **je**); the formal form is **alstublieft**. Both are also said when handing something over.                                                  |
| I’m studying the financial market.                   | Ik ben de financiële markt aan het bestuderen.               | **zijn aan het** + infinitive = "busy doing", the Dutch "-ing" form. **financiële** gets **-e** before a **de**-word; the two dots show the **e** is pronounced separately.            |
| I graduated in engineering.                          | Ik ben afgestudeerd in engineering.                          | **afstuderen** takes **zijn** in the perfect tense, and the **ge-** goes inside the separable verb: **af·ge·studeerd**.                                                                |
| Have you read Nietzsche’s books?                     | Heb je de boeken van Nietzsche gelezen?                      | Yes/no question: verb first, participle **gelezen** at the end. When **je** comes after the verb, the **-t** drops: **heb je**, not **hebt je**. Possession is usually **de ... van**. |
| I like movies, theater and restaurants.              | Ik hou van films, theater en restaurants.                    | **houden van** again. Many loanwords form their plural with **-s**: **films**, **restaurants**.                                                                                        |

# 3 – Asking for Help

Hoofdstuk 3 – Hulp vragen

| ?                                                 | !                                                    | +                                                                                                                                                                    |
|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I’m looking for museums, can you help me?         | Ik ben op zoek naar musea, kun je me helpen?         | **op zoek zijn naar** = to be looking for (fixed phrase). Modal **kun** + infinitive **helpen** at the end; the **-t** of **kunt** drops before **je**.              |
| Good afternoon, can you help me take a picture?   | Goedemiddag, kun je me helpen een foto te maken?     | Dutch "makes" a photo: **een foto maken**. After **helpen**, the second verb comes with **te** at the end.                                                           |
| How do I get to this location on the map?         | Hoe kom ik naar deze plek op de kaart?               | **Hoe kom ik naar/bij ...?** is the standard way to ask the way. **plek** is a **de**-word, so "this" is **deze** (with **het**-words: **dit**).                     |
| Where can I find an ATM?                          | Waar kan ik een geldautomaat vinden?                 | The V2 rule: the conjugated verb (**kan**) is the second element, and the infinitive (**vinden**) goes to the very end. Paying or withdrawing by card is **pinnen**. |
| What time is it?                                  | Hoe laat is het?                                     | Literally "how late is it?". Answer: **Het is drie uur** (it's three o'clock).                                                                                       |
| Where can I get a cab?                            | Waar kan ik een taxi nemen?                          | Transport is "taken" with **nemen**, as in English. Same modal pattern: **kan ... nemen**.                                                                           |
| Is this place dangerous at night?                 | Is deze plaats ‘s nachts gevaarlijk?                 | **'s nachts** (at night) is an old "of the night" form, like **'s morgens** and **'s avonds**. Time words come before the adjective at the end.                      |
| How can I take the subway?                        | Hoe kan ik de metro nemen?                           | **nemen** again for transport. Question word first, then the verb: **Hoe kan ik**.                                                                                   |
| How can I get on the bus?                         | Hoe kan ik in de bus stappen?                        | **instappen** (separable) = get on; here split as **in de bus stappen**. Getting off is **uitstappen**.                                                              |
| How can I get on the train?                       | Hoe kan ik in de trein stappen?                      | Same pattern as the bus. A ticket is **een kaartje**; the station is **het station**.                                                                                |
| Where is the airport?                             | Waar is het vliegveld?                               | **het vliegveld** (literally "fly-field") is a **het**-word; **de luchthaven** is the more formal word.                                                              |
| I need access to the internet, could you help me? | Ik heb toegang tot internet nodig, kan je me helpen? | **nodig hebben** = to need: **ik heb ... nodig**, with **nodig** at the end of the clause. **kan je** is a common spoken variant of **kun je**.                      |
| Can I make a call?                                | Kan ik bellen?                                       | **bellen** = to phone (and to ring a bell). To call someone: **iemand bellen** or separable **opbellen** (**ik bel je op**).                                         |
| Where can I find a market?                        | Waar kan ik een markt vinden?                        | **de markt**: most Dutch towns hold a weekly open-air market. V2 again: **kan** second, **vinden** last.                                                             |
| I need to find a pharmacy.                        | Ik moet een apotheek zoeken.                         | **moeten** = must/have to, with the infinitive at the end. **zoeken** is "look for"; "find" is **vinden**.                                                           |
| I need a hospital.                                | Ik heb een ziekenhuis nodig.                         | **nodig hebben** again. **het ziekenhuis** is literally "sick-house".                                                                                                |

# 4 – Making Friends

Hoofdstuk 4 – Vrienden maken

| ?                                               | !                                                   | +                                                                                                                                                        |
|-------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| My name is John, what’s your name?              | Mijn naam is John, hoe heet je?                     | **Hoe heet je?** (literally "how are you called?") is the most natural way to ask someone's name.                                                        |
| Very nice to meet you.                          | Erg leuk je te ontmoeten.                           | **erg** = very in everyday speech (as an adjective on its own it means "bad"). **heel** works too.                                                       |
| Are you free tonight?                           | Ben je vrij vanavond?                               | Inversion in a question: verb first, then **je**. **vanavond** = this evening; **Ben je vanavond vrij?** is the more standard order.                     |
| I do not have a girlfriend.                     | Ik heb geen vriendin.                               | **vriendin** means girlfriend or female friend; context decides. **geen** again for a noun.                                                              |
| I’m still practicing the language.              | Ik ben nog steeds de taal aan het oefenen.          | **aan het** + infinitive again for an ongoing action. **nog steeds** = still.                                                                            |
| I bought a shirt yesterday.                     | Ik heb gisteren een shirt gekocht.                  | Perfect tense with **hebben**: **heb ... gekocht** (from irregular **kopen**). Dutch uses this tense for most past events in speech.                     |
| How many languages do you speak?                | Hoeveel talen spreek je?                            | Question word, verb, subject. **taal → talen**: one **a** in the plural still sounds long.                                                               |
| Can you recommend a restaurant?                 | Kun je me een restaurant aanraden?                  | **aanraden** = to recommend (separable: **ik raad het aan**); after a modal it stays whole at the end. **aanbevelen** is more formal.                    |
| What are you doing tonight?                     | Wat doe je vanavond?                                | The Dutch present tense covers plans and "-ing" meanings; no special form is needed.                                                                     |
| I have two cats and one dog.                    | Ik heb twee katten en een hond.                     | **een** means both "a" and "one"; write **één** when stressing "one". **kat → katten**: the consonant doubles to keep the vowel short.                   |
| Do you know Mary?                               | Ken je Mary?                                        | **kennen** = know a person or place; **weten** = know a fact (**Weet je waar ze woont?**).                                                               |
| What are your hobbies?                          | Wat zijn je hobby's?                                | Words ending in a vowel take **'s** in the plural: **hobby's**, **foto's**, **auto's**.                                                                  |
| How long have you lived here?                   | Hoe lang woon je hier al?                           | For something still going on, Dutch uses the present tense + **al**: literally "how long live you here already?".                                        |
| Your kids have great behavior.                  | Je kinderen gedragen zich heel goed.                | **zich gedragen** = to behave, a reflexive verb: **ik gedraag me**, **zij gedragen zich**.                                                               |
| What is the name of the company where you work? | Wat is de naam van het bedrijf waar u werkt?        | After **waar** the clause is subordinate, so the verb goes to the end: **waar u werkt**. **u** is formal "you"; with a friend say **waar je werkt**.     |
| Are you going to work tomorrow?                 | Ga jij morgen werken?                               | **gaan** + infinitive = "going to". **jij** is the stressed form of **je**, putting emphasis on "you".                                                   |
| As soon as possible.                            | Zo spoedig mogelijk.                                | **zo ... mogelijk** = as ... as possible. **spoedig** is formal; in daily speech say **zo snel mogelijk**.                                               |
| Goodbye! Let’s keep in touch.                   | Tot ziens! Laten we contact houden.                 | **Laten we** + infinitive at the end = "let's". **Tot ziens** is the normal goodbye; **vaarwel** sounds like parting forever.                            |
| Bye! See you later.                             | Doei! Tot later.                                    | **Doei** is very informal (also **dag**). **tot** + a time = "see you ...": **tot straks**, **tot morgen**.                                              |
| I have a gift for you.                          | Ik heb een geschenk voor je.                        | **geschenk** is rather formal; in daily speech people say **cadeau** or **cadeautje**.                                                                   |
| I have two tickets for tomorrow’s game.         | Ik heb twee kaartjes voor de wedstrijd van morgen.  | **kaartje** is the diminutive of **kaart**; diminutives are very common and always **het**-words. "Tomorrow's game" becomes **de wedstrijd van morgen**. |
| We can eat Italian or Chinese food.             | We kunnen Italiaans of Chinees eten.                | **eten** is both the verb "eat" and the noun "food", so **Italiaans eten** covers "Italian food".                                                        |
| Can you hold this for me?                       | Kun je dit voor mij vasthouden?                     | **vasthouden** = hold (separable: **ik houd het vast**); after **kun** it stays together at the end.                                                     |
| I have to leave this afternoon.                 | Ik moet vanmiddag vertrekken.                       | **van** + part of day = this ...: **vanochtend**, **vanmiddag**, **vanavond**, **vannacht**.                                                             |
| Tomorrow is my birthday, I’m having a party.    | Morgen is mijn verjaardag, ik geef een feestje.     | You "give" a party: **een feestje geven**. V2 after a fronted word: **Morgen is**, verb right after the first element.                                   |
| Do you want to come to my party?                | Wil je naar mijn feestje komen?                     | **willen** + infinitive at the end. **naar** = to (a place or event).                                                                                    |
| I have to go, it’s late.                        | Ik moet gaan, het is laat.                          | Very common short version: **Ik moet weg** (the verb of movement can be dropped after a modal).                                                          |
| I think it’s going to rain tomorrow.            | Ik denk dat het morgen gaat regenen.                | **dat** starts a subordinate clause, so the verbs move to the end: **dat het morgen gaat regenen**.                                                      |
| How is the weather here in the city?            | Hoe is het weer hier in de stad?                    | **het weer** = the weather; don't confuse it with **weer** = again.                                                                                      |
| Follow me, I’ll show you something cool.        | Volg mij, ik zal je iets cools laten zien.          | **iets** + adjective takes **-s**: **iets cools**, **iets leuks**. **laten zien** (literally "let see") = show, at the end.                              |
| Do you want to go to the movies?                | Wil je naar de film gaan?                           | **naar de film gaan** = go to the movies; also **naar de bioscoop**.                                                                                     |
| How long have you been in town?                 | Hoe lang ben je al in de stad?                      | Present tense + **al** again for "have been": "how long are you already in town?".                                                                       |
| What is your favorite food?                     | Wat is je favoriete eten?                           | After a possessive like **je** or **mijn**, the adjective always takes **-e**, even with a **het**-word: **je favoriete eten**. Also **lievelingseten**. |
| I like Japanese and Chinese food.               | Ik hou van Japans en Chinees eten.                  | No **-e** here: **eten** is a **het**-word used without an article, so the adjective stays bare.                                                         |
| Can I ask you a question?                       | Mag ik je een vraag stellen?                        | You "put" a question: **een vraag stellen**. **Mag ik** (may I) is the polite way to ask permission.                                                     |
| Thank you for asking, I’m fine, and you?        | Bedankt voor het vragen, het gaat goed, en met jou? | **En met jou?** is short for **hoe gaat het met jou?**; **jou** is the stressed object form of **jij**.                                                  |
| How is the weather?                             | Hoe is het weer?                                    | Also common: **Wat voor weer is het?** (what kind of weather is it?).                                                                                    |

# 5 – Hotel

Hoofdstuk 5 – Hotel

| ?                                     | !                                             | +                                                                                                                         |
|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I have a reservation.                 | Ik heb een reservering.                       | **de reservering** is a de-word; in Belgium you will also hear **de reservatie**.                                         |
| I’d like to check in, please.         | Ik zou graag willen inchecken.                | **zou graag willen** is the extra-polite "would like"; the infinitive **inchecken** goes to the very end of the sentence. |
| Are there rooms available?            | Zijn er kamers beschikbaar?                   | **er** here means "there" in "there are"; questions start with the verb, so **zijn** comes first.                         |
| How much is the rate?                 | Hoeveel kost het tarief?                      | Dutch asks what something "costs": **Hoeveel kost …?** is the standard way to ask a price.                                |
| Where is my room?                     | Waar is mijn kamer?                           | Question word first, then the verb: **Waar is …?** works for any place you are looking for.                               |
| What’s the wi-fi password?            | Wat is het wifi-wachtwoord?                   | In a compound the last word decides de/het: **het wachtwoord**, so **het wifi-wachtwoord**.                               |
| Where is the restaurant?              | Waar is het restaurant?                       | **het restaurant** is a het-word; the stress falls on the last syllable.                                                  |
| Where is the pool?                    | Waar is het zwembad?                          | Literally "swim bath": **zwemmen** (to swim) + **het bad**.                                                               |
| Do you have a gym in the hotel?       | Hebben jullie een fitnessruimte in het hotel? | Use **jullie** (plural you) when asking staff about their hotel; you can also just say **de fitness**.                    |
| I lost the key to my room.            | Ik ben de sleutel van mijn kamer kwijt.       | Idiom: **kwijt zijn** = to have lost; Dutch says "I am the key lost" rather than using a past tense.                      |
| I need more pillows.                  | Ik heb meer kussens nodig.                    | **nodig hebben** = to need: **hebben** sits in second place and **nodig** goes to the end.                                |
| I need another sheet.                 | Ik heb nog een laken nodig.                   | **nog een** = one more / another; **het laken** is a bed sheet (**het blad** is a sheet of paper or a leaf).              |
| I would like to ask for room service. | Ik wil graag om roomservice vragen.           | **vragen om** = to ask for; **graag** after a verb turns "want" into a polite "would like".                               |
| What is the check-out time?           | Wat is de uitchecktijd?                       | **de tijd** is a de-word, so the whole compound **de uitchecktijd** is too.                                               |
| I want to check it out.               | Ik wil het bekijken.                          | Modal verb + infinitive: **wil** stays second and **bekijken** (to look at) moves to the end.                             |
| Until what time is breakfast?         | Tot hoe laat is het ontbijt?                  | **hoe laat** ("how late") is how Dutch asks "what time"; **tot** = until.                                                 |
| Is breakfast included?                | Is het ontbijt inbegrepen?                    | Dutch usually keeps the article with meals: **het ontbijt**, **de lunch**, **het avondeten**.                             |
| I need to extend my reservation.      | Ik moet mijn reservering verlengen.           | **moeten** = must / need to; the infinitive **verlengen** closes the sentence, like with every modal verb.                |

# 6 – Shopping

Hoofdstuk 6 – Winkelen

| ?                                         | !                                           | +                                                                                                                |
|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Can I help you?                           | Kan ik u helpen?                            | **u** is the formal "you", what shop staff use with customers; with a friend it would be **je**.                 |
| I’m looking for a small size black dress. | Ik zoek een zwarte jurk in een kleine maat. | **de maat** = size; the adjective takes **-e** before a de-word: **een zwarte jurk**.                            |
| Let me know if you decide anything.       | Laat het me weten als je iets beslist.      | **als** starts a subordinate clause, so its verb **beslist** goes to the end.                                    |
| Where can I find ...?                     | Waar kan ik ... vinden?                     | Fill in the item between **ik** and **vinden**: the infinitive always stays at the end.                          |
| How much?                                 | Hoeveel?                                    | Always written as one word: **hoeveel**.                                                                         |
| Does this outfit have a discount?         | Zit er korting op deze outfit?              | Dutch says discount "sits on" an item: **Er zit korting op**; no article is needed before **korting**.           |
| Do you accept credit cards?               | Accepteert u creditcards?                   | With **u** the verb takes **-t** even in questions: **accepteert u**, but **accepteer je**.                      |
| Is it another size?                       | Is het een andere maat?                     | **ander** gets **-e** after **een** before a de-word: **een andere maat**.                                       |
| I want to buy this product.               | Ik wil dit product kopen.                   | **dit** is "this" for het-words (**het product**); **deze** is for de-words.                                     |
| Can I return this?                        | Kan ik dit terugbrengen?                    | Separable verb **terug·brengen**: without a modal it splits, **Ik breng het terug**.                             |
| Can I try this one?                       | Kan ik deze proberen?                       | **deze** on its own means "this one"; for clothes you can also say **passen** (to try on).                       |
| Where’s the dressing room?                | Waar is de paskamer?                        | **de paskamer** comes from **passen** (to try on); **de kleedkamer** is a changing room at a gym or theatre.     |
| This one looks good on you.               | Deze staat je goed.                         | Idiom: clothes "stand" you well, **Dat staat je goed**; **er goed uitzien** just means to look good.             |
| Is there something cheaper?               | Is er iets goedkopers?                      | After **iets** an adjective takes **-s**: **iets goedkopers**, **iets lekkers**, **iets nieuws**.                |
| Do you have another color?                | Heb je een andere kleur?                    | Inversion in questions: **je hebt** becomes **heb je**, and the **-t** drops.                                    |
| Do you have a product on sale?            | Heeft u een product in de aanbieding?       | **in de aanbieding** = on special offer; a clearance sale is **de uitverkoop**.                                  |
| I’m not interested.                       | Ik ben niet geïnteresseerd.                 | **niet** negates the adjective here; the diaeresis in **geïnteresseerd** shows that e and i are said separately. |
| Can you wrap it as a gift?                | Kun je het als cadeau inpakken?             | **in·pakken** is the word for gift-wrapping; **als cadeau** = as a gift.                                         |
| I will pay in cash.                       | Ik zal contant betalen.                     | **zullen** + infinitive is the future, but Dutch often just uses the present: **Ik betaal contant**.             |
| I’m just looking, thank you.              | Ik kijk alleen even rond, dank u.           | Separable **rond·kijken** (to look around): **rond** goes to the end; **even** softens the sentence.             |
| I will be back later.                     | Ik kom later terug.                         | The present tense often covers the future; separable **terug·komen** puts **terug** at the end.                  |
| Clothing store                            | Kledingwinkel                               | **de winkel** = shop; it sits at the end of all these compounds and makes them de-words.                         |
| Electronics store                         | Elektronicawinkel                           | Compounds are written as one word in Dutch, never as two separate words.                                         |
| The shoe store                            | De schoenenwinkel                           | Note the plural inside the compound: **schoenen** + **winkel**.                                                  |
| Fragrance store.                          | Parfumerie.                                 | **de parfumerie** sells perfume and cosmetics; the word comes straight from French.                              |
| Souvenir shop                             | Souvenirwinkel                              | **het souvenir** is a het-word, but the compound takes **de** from **winkel**.                                   |

# 7 – Restaurant

Hoofdstuk 7 – Restaurant

| ?                                                 | !                                                    | +                                                                                                               |
|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Waiter and Waitress                               | Ober en serveerster                                  | Nouns are not capitalised in Dutch; the feminine ending **-ster** also gives **de kapster** (hairdresser).      |
| I’d like a table for two, please.                 | Ik wil graag een tafel voor twee, alsjeblieft.       | **alsjeblieft** is informal, **alstublieft** formal; both also mean "here you are" when handing something over. |
| Can you bring me the menu, please?                | Kun je me alsjeblieft het menu brengen?              | The short object **me** comes right after the subject; **brengen** goes to the end.                             |
| Do you have vegetarian food?                      | Heb je vegetarisch eten?                             | No **-e** on the adjective: **eten** is a het-word used without an article.                                     |
| I want to make my request, please.                | Ik wil graag bestellen.                              | **bestellen** = to order; this is the phrase to use when you are ready to order.                                |
| Can you bring the dessert menu, please?           | Kun je alsjeblieft het dessertmenu brengen?          | **alsjeblieft** can sit in the middle of the sentence, before the object.                                       |
| Do you have a menu for drinks?                    | Heb je een drankenkaart?                             | **de kaart** (card) is often used for menus: **de menukaart**, **de wijnkaart**, **de drankenkaart**.           |
| Excuse me, there is a problem with my food.       | Pardon, er is een probleem met mijn eten.            | **er is** = there is; **pardon** is the usual way to get someone's attention politely.                          |
| I’d like to speak to the manager, please.         | Ik zou graag de manager willen spreken, alsjeblieft. | With **zou ... willen** the two infinitives end the sentence: **willen spreken**.                               |
| What is the specialty of the restaurant?          | Wat is de specialiteit van het restaurant?           | You can also ask **Wat is de specialiteit van het huis?** ("of the house").                                     |
| I want to make a reservation.                     | Ik wil een reservering maken.                        | V2 again: **wil** second, **maken** at the end; in Belgium **reserveren** alone is common.                      |
| I want a wine recommendation please.              | Ik wil graag een wijnaanbeveling.                    | Long compound: **wijn** + **aanbeveling**; a lighter option is **Kunt u een wijn aanbevelen?**                  |
| I’m looking for an ice cream parlor.              | Ik ben op zoek naar een ijssalon.                    | **op zoek zijn naar** = to be looking for; same meaning as **zoeken**.                                          |
| Where can I have a good coffee?                   | Waar kan ik een goede koffie krijgen?                | **krijgen** = to get; **goed** takes **-e** before the de-word **koffie**.                                      |
| No sugar please.                                  | Geen suiker alsjeblieft.                             | **geen** replaces "no / not any" before a noun; **niet** is for verbs and adjectives.                           |
| With sugar, please.                               | Met suiker, alsjeblieft.                             | **met** = with; the opposite is **zonder** (without).                                                           |
| Do you have salt?                                 | Heb je zout?                                         | **het zout** is a het-word; "salty" is **zout** too, as in **Het is te zout**.                                  |
| Do you have olive oil?                            | Heb je olijfolie?                                    | The **ij** in **olijf** sounds like the "ay" in "day" said with wide lips.                                      |
| What is the most famous restaurant in the city?   | Wat is het beroemdste restaurant in de stad?         | Superlative: add **-st** (**beroemdst**) plus the usual **-e** after **het**.                                   |
| I want to order this pasta dish with spicy sauce. | Ik wil dit pastagerecht met pittige saus bestellen.  | **het gerecht** makes it **dit pastagerecht**; **pittig** = spicy-hot, not just flavoured.                      |
| What sauces are available?                        | Welke sauzen zijn er beschikbaar?                    | **welke** = which/what before a plural; note the plural **saus** to **sauzen** (s becomes z).                   |
| I want some strawberry juice.                     | Ik wil wat aardbeiensap.                             | **wat** before a noun means "some"; **het sap** = juice.                                                        |
| Do you have soda?                                 | Heb je frisdrank?                                    | **de frisdrank** is the general word for soft drinks; "soda" is not used this way in Dutch.                     |
| I want some diet drink.                           | Ik wil een light frisdrank.                          | Dutch uses the English word **light** for diet versions: **cola light**, **light frisdrank**.                   |
| Do you have any alcoholic beverages?              | Heb je alcoholische dranken?                         | No word for "any" is needed in questions; **de drank** pluralises to **dranken**.                               |
| I am satisfied.                                   | Ik ben tevreden.                                     | **tevreden** = satisfied, content; to say you are full after a meal, use **Ik zit vol**.                        |
| Does it contain milk?                             | Bevat het melk?                                      | **bevatten** = to contain; Dutch needs the subject **het**, it cannot be left out like in "Contains milk?".     |
| I have a lactose allergy.                         | Ik heb een lactose-allergie.                         | A hyphen separates two vowels that would otherwise run together: **lactose-allergie**.                          |
| I would like to pay, please.                      | Ik wil graag betalen.                                | A key restaurant phrase; you can also say **Mag ik de rekening?** (May I have the bill?).                       |
| Could you bring the credit card machine?          | Kun je het pinapparaat brengen?                      | **pinnen** = to pay by card in the Netherlands, hence **het pinapparaat**.                                      |
| Is the service included?                          | Is de service inbegrepen?                            | Restaurant service is also called **de bediening**; tipping is **fooi geven**.                                  |

# 9 – Airport and Flight

Hoofdstuk 9 – Luchthaven en vlucht

| ?                                            | !                                              | +                                                                                                                                          |
|----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I want to check in.                          | Ik wil inchecken.                              | **Willen** is a modal verb: the main verb goes to the end as an infinitive. **Inchecken** is separable: "I check in" is **ik check in**.   |
| What is the flight schedule?                 | Wat is het vluchtschema?                       | Dutch glues nouns into one word: **vlucht** + **schema**. A compound takes the article of its last part, so **het** (from **het schema**). |
| Was there a delay for my flight?             | Had mijn vlucht vertraging?                    | Dutch says a flight "has delay": **vertraging hebben**, with no article. In a yes/no question the verb comes first.                        |
| What is my gate?                             | Wat is mijn gate?                              | Dutch airports simply use the English word **gate** (pronounced as in English).                                                            |
| Where is the bathroom?                       | Waar is het toilet?                            | Ask for **het toilet** (or informally **de wc**, said "way-say"), not **de badkamer**, which is a room with a bath or shower.              |
| Do you have a car rental service?            | Heeft u een autoverhuurservice?                | **U** is the polite "you" and takes **heeft**, like he/she. Informal would be **heb je**: the verb loses its -t when **je** follows it.    |
| I prefer the window seat.                    | Ik geef de voorkeur aan de stoel bij het raam. | Literally: "I give the preference to the seat by the window." Shorter and more everyday: **Ik zit liever bij het raam**.                   |
| I need help with luggage.                    | Ik heb hulp nodig met bagage.                  | **Nodig hebben** = to need: **hebben** is the verb and **nodig** comes after the object (**ik heb hulp nodig**).                           |
| I can not find my seat.                      | Ik kan mijn stoel niet vinden.                 | **Niet** comes after the object (**mijn stoel**) and before the infinitive at the end.                                                     |
| Would you like a drink please?               | Wilt u een drankje?                            | **Drankje** is a diminutive (-je), and all diminutives are **het** words. Dutch doesn't put **alsjeblieft** in an offer like this.         |
| How much time do we have to fly?             | Hoe lang moeten we vliegen?                    | **Hoe lang** (how long) is the natural way to ask about duration. **We** is the unstressed form of **wij**.                                |
| Could you bring a pillow please?             | Kunt u mij alstublieft een kussen brengen?     | **Brengen** = to bring (to someone); **meenemen** means to take along. **Alstublieft** is the polite form that goes with **u**.            |
| I’d like to order a snack.                   | Ik wil graag een snack bestellen.              | **Graag** softens a request: **ik wil graag** = I'd like. It is the go-to polite way to order.                                             |
| I do not want to go near the emergency exit. | Ik wil niet bij de nooduitgang komen.          | **Nood** (emergency) + **uitgang** (exit). Note **komen** where English says "go": you come near a place in Dutch.                         |
| Do you offer a headset?                      | Biedt u een hoofdtelefoon aan?                 | Separable verb **aan·bieden** (to offer): the **aan** part jumps to the end. In the Netherlands **koptelefoon** is the more common word.   |

# 11 – Directions

Hoofdstuk 11 – Richtingen

| ?                                                                         | !                                                                   | +                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Go straight on for 100 meters and then turn right.                        | Ga 100 meter rechtdoor en sla dan rechtsaf.                         | The command form is just the verb stem: **ga**, **sla**. **Rechtsaf/linksaf slaan** = turn right/left. After a number, **meter** stays singular.                                   |
| It’s two blocks from here.                                                | Het is twee straten hiervandaan.                                    | Dutch cities don't count "blocks"; you count streets (**straten**). **Hiervandaan** = from here.                                                                                   |
| It is next to the square.                                                 | Het ligt naast het plein.                                           | For where things are, Dutch likes **liggen** (lie), **staan** (stand) or **zitten** (sit) instead of **zijn**.                                                                     |
| Walk in this direction until you find a statue.                           | Loop in deze richting totdat je een standbeeld vindt.               | **Totdat** starts a subordinate clause, so the verb **vindt** moves to the end. **Lopen** means to walk (not to run).                                                              |
| It’s a mile away.                                                         | Het is anderhalve kilometer verderop.                               | Distances are given in kilometres: **anderhalve** = one and a half. **Verderop** = further on.                                                                                     |
| It is about a thirty minute walk.                                         | Het is ongeveer dertig minuten lopen.                               | Literally: "It is about thirty minutes walking." The infinitive **lopen** works like an "-ing" word here.                                                                          |
| What is the direction to get to the stadium?                              | Hoe kom ik bij het stadion?                                         | The standard way to ask for directions: **Hoe kom ik bij/naar ...?** Verb second, then the subject, as in any question word question.                                              |
| Where can I find a mall?                                                  | Waar kan ik een winkelcentrum vinden?                               | V2 rule: in a main clause the conjugated verb is always the second element. **Waar** is first, so **kan** comes before the subject, and the infinitive **vinden** goes to the end. |
| You can walk for 30 minutes or catch an Uber for approximately 4 minutes. | Je kunt 30 minuten lopen of ongeveer 4 minuten met een Uber rijden. | You travel **met** a vehicle: **met de bus**, **met een Uber**. **Rijden** = to ride/drive.                                                                                        |
| Careful, it’s a long walk.                                                | Pas op, het is een lange wandeling.                                 | **Op·passen** (to watch out) is separable: **pas op!** Adjective ending: **een lange wandeling** (de-word, so -e).                                                                 |
| How far is the museum from here?                                          | Hoe ver is het museum hier vandaan?                                 | **Hier ... vandaan** = from here; it can be split around other words or written together (**hiervandaan**).                                                                        |
| Can I get there with the raft?                                            | Kan ik daar komen met het vlot?                                     | **Daar komen** = get there. Dutch often puts the **met** phrase after the infinitive in speech, as here.                                                                           |
| Where can I rent bikes?                                                   | Waar kan ik fietsen huren?                                          | **Fiets** (bike) has plural **fietsen**, which is also the verb "to cycle".                                                                                                        |
| I’m looking for tour buses.                                               | Ik zoek tourbussen.                                                 | **Zoeken** = to look for, with no extra word for "for". Plural **bus → bussen**: the s doubles to keep the u short.                                                                |
| Go straight on for two blocks then turn left into the square.             | Ga twee straten rechtdoor en sla dan linksaf het plein op.          | **Het plein op**: the direction word **op** comes after the place when you move onto it.                                                                                           |
| Traffic is very bad at this time.                                         | Het verkeer is op dit moment erg druk.                              | Heavy traffic is **druk** (busy), not **slecht**. **Erg** is an everyday word for "very".                                                                                          |
| There are two ways to get there.                                          | Er zijn twee manieren om er te komen.                               | **Er zijn** = there are. **Om ... te** + infinitive expresses "to/in order to"; the second **er** means "there".                                                                   |
| By subway or bus.                                                         | Met de metro of bus.                                                | Again **met** for the means of transport: **met de metro**, **met de bus**.                                                                                                        |
| I recommend the first one.                                                | Ik raad de eerste aan.                                              | Separable verb **aan·raden** (to recommend): **ik raad ... aan**.                                                                                                                  |

Hoofdstuk 12 – Naar de dokter gaan

|----------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I want to make an appointment.                     | Ik wil een afspraak maken.                        | **Een afspraak maken** = make an appointment; modal **wil** + infinitive at the end.                                                     |
| Do you have a schedule for Tuesday?                | Heeft u dinsdag nog tijd?                         | Literally: "Do you still have time on Tuesday?" **Nog** (still) is very common here. Days of the week have no capital in Dutch.          |
| What’s the doctor’s name?                          | Hoe heet de dokter?                               | Dutch asks "How is the doctor called?" with **heten**: **Hoe heet je?** = What's your name?                                              |
| How much does the service cost?                    | Hoeveel kost de service?                          | No helper verb like "does": the main verb **kost** simply comes right after **hoeveel**.                                                 |
| I’m having a headache.                             | Ik heb hoofdpijn.                                 | Aches are **pijn** compounds used with **hebben** and no article: **hoofd** (head) + **pijn**.                                           |
| I have a stomachache.                              | Ik heb buikpijn.                                  | **Buik** = belly; this is the usual word for stomach ache.                                                                               |
| I’m sick and nauseated.                            | Ik ben ziek en misselijk.                         | **Misselijk** = feeling sick to your stomach. Feelings like this use **zijn**, not **hebben**.                                           |
| I have a backache.                                 | Ik heb rugpijn.                                   | **Rug** = back. Same pattern: **ik heb** + body part + **pijn**.                                                                         |
| I have toothache.                                  | Ik heb tandpijn.                                  | **Tand** = tooth; you will also hear **kiespijn** (molar ache).                                                                          |
| I think I have a fever.                            | Ik denk dat ik koorts heb.                        | **Dat** starts a subordinate clause, so **heb** moves to the very end.                                                                   |
| I had a fever all night.                           | Ik heb de hele nacht koorts gehad.                | Dutch often uses the perfect for the past: **heb ... gehad**, with the participle at the end. **Hele** gets -e before a de-word.         |
| I’ve been feeling like this for five days.         | Ik voel me al vijf dagen zo.                      | Dutch uses the present + **al** where English says "have been ... for". **Zich voelen** is reflexive: **ik voel me**.                    |
| How is the treatment?                              | Hoe is de behandeling?                            | **Behandeling** (treatment) comes from **behandelen**; nouns in **-ing** are always **de** words.                                        |
| How soon will I be fully healed?                   | Hoe snel zal ik volledig genezen zijn?            | **Zullen** (will) sends the rest to the end: **genezen zijn**. Everyday alternative: **Wanneer ben ik weer beter?**                      |
| Is this disease contagious?                        | Is deze ziekte besmettelijk?                      | **Deze** = this for de-words; with a het-word it becomes **dit**.                                                                        |
| Is this disease serious?                           | Is deze ziekte ernstig?                           | **Ernstig** = serious (for illness or problems); **serieus** is used more for people.                                                    |
| Can I exchange this medicine for another?          | Kan ik dit medicijn vervangen door een ander?     | **Dit**, because **medicijn** is a het-word. **Vervangen door** = replace with; **een ander** has no -e because it refers to a het-word. |
| No, I do not have any allergies.                   | Nee, ik heb geen allergieën.                      | **Geen** replaces "not a/not any" before a noun with no article; **niet** is for everything else.                                        |

| Is there any alternative treatment?                | Is er een alternatieve behandeling?               | **Is er** = is there. **Alternatief** becomes **alternatieve** with the -e ending (f changes to v).                                      |
| I want to make a new appointment with doctor John. | Ik wil een nieuwe afspraak maken met dokter John. | **Nieuwe** gets -e before a de-word. Titles like **dokter** take no capital before a name.                                               |

# 13 – Work

Hoofdstuk 13 – Werk

| ?                                                                   | !                                                                       | +                                                                                                                                                       |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I need to finish the project by tomorrow.                           | Ik moet het project morgen afmaken.                                     | **Moeten** + infinitive at the end. **Af·maken** (to finish) is separable: **ik maak het project af**.                                                  |
| Yes, tomorrow is my deadline.                                       | Ja, morgen is mijn deadline.                                            | **Ja** sits outside the sentence and doesn't count for word order. **Deadline** is borrowed from English.                                               |
| I have over a hundred unread emails.                                | Ik heb meer dan honderd ongelezen e-mails.                              | **Meer dan** = more than. **Honderd** needs no "a/one". **Ongelezen** = un- + read (participle used as an adjective).                                   |
| I have a business dinner today.                                     | Ik heb vandaag een zakendiner.                                          | Time comes before the object: **vandaag** before **een zakendiner**. **Zaken** = business.                                                              |
| I have a meeting at three o’clock.                                  | Ik heb een vergadering om drie uur.                                     | Clock times use **om**: **om drie uur**. **Uur** stays singular after a number.                                                                         |
| Let’s schedule a meeting with the board.                            | Laten we een vergadering plannen met het bestuur.                       | **Laten we** + infinitive = let's. A company's board is **het bestuur** (**het bord** is a plate or a sign board).                                      |
| The director asked me for a new updated report.                     | De directeur vroeg me om een nieuw bijgewerkt rapport.                  | **Vragen om** = ask for. **Nieuw** and **bijgewerkt** have no -e: **een** + het-word (**het rapport**).                                                 |
| We need to optimize our routines.                                   | We moeten onze routines optimaliseren.                                  | **Onze** = our with de-words and plurals; with a het-word it is **ons** (**ons bedrijf**).                                                              |
| We need to schedule a new interview.                                | We moeten een nieuw sollicitatiegesprek plannen.                        | A job interview is a **sollicitatiegesprek** (from **solliciteren**, to apply); **interview** is mostly for the media.                                  |
| I need to hire an intern.                                           | Ik moet een stagiair aannemen.                                          | You hire people with **aannemen** (separable); **huren** is for renting things. An internship is **een stage**.                                         |
| This project is scheduled for two years.                            | Dit project staat gepland voor twee jaar.                               | **Gepland staan** = be scheduled, again with **staan**. **Jaar** stays singular after a number.                                                         |
| Our marketing department is updating the company’s visual identity. | Onze marketingafdeling werkt de visuele identiteit van het bedrijf bij. | Separable **bij·werken** (to update): **werkt ... bij**. Dutch has no "-ing" tense; the simple present covers "is updating".                            |
| The finance department has created a new cash flow report.          | De financiële afdeling heeft een nieuw kasstroomverslag opgesteld.      | Perfect with **hebben** + participle at the end: **heeft ... opgesteld** (from separable **op·stellen**, to draw up).                                   |
| Tomorrow the new employees arrive.                                  | Morgen komen de nieuwe medewerkers aan.                                 | V2: **morgen** first, then the verb **komen**, then the subject. Separable **aan·komen**: **aan** goes to the end.                                      |
| The president of the company requested a meeting.                   | De president-directeur van het bedrijf vroeg om een vergadering.        | The head of a company is a **president-directeur** or **directeur**; **president** alone suggests a country. **Vroeg** is the past tense of **vragen**. |
| You need to keep the system up to date.                             | U moet het systeem up-to-date houden.                                   | **Houden** = keep; it goes to the end after the modal **moet**. **Het systeem** is a het-word.                                                          |
| Today begins phase two of the project.                              | Vandaag begint fase twee van het project.                               | Same word order as the English, thanks to V2: time first, verb second, subject after.                                                                   |

# 14 – Other Phrases

Hoofdstuk 14 – Andere zinnen

| ?                                                                                | !                                                                                                 | +                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I hope you and your wife have a good trip.                                       | Ik hoop dat jij en je vrouw een goede reis hebben.                                                | **dat** ("that") starts a subordinate clause, which sends the verb to the very end: **... een goede reis hebben**. Note the stressed **jij** next to the unstressed **je** ("your").                               |
| Thanks for your help.                                                            | Bedankt voor je hulp.                                                                             | **Bedankt** is the everyday "thanks"; **je** is informal "your" (formal: **uw hulp**).                                                                                                                             |
| I would like to walk with you.                                                   | Ik zou graag met je willen wandelen.                                                              | **zou ... willen** = "would like"; **graag** adds "gladly". The infinitives **willen wandelen** stack up at the end of the sentence.                                                                               |
| I do not know how to use this.                                                   | Ik weet niet hoe ik dit moet gebruiken.                                                           | Dutch says "how I this must use": after **hoe** the verbs go to the end (**moet gebruiken**). English "how to" needs a full clause with a subject.                                                                 |
| I can not help you.                                                              | Ik kan je niet helpen.                                                                            | Modal verb + infinitive: **kan** stays second, **helpen** goes to the end. **niet** comes after the object **je**.                                                                                                 |
| If you need my help, please tell me.                                             | Als je mijn hulp nodig hebt, vertel het me dan.                                                   | **iets nodig hebben** = "to need something"; inside the **als** clause **hebt** goes to the end. **dan** ("then") softens the request.                                                                             |
| I need to change clothes.                                                        | Ik moet me omkleden.                                                                              | **zich omkleden** is a reflexive separable verb: **ik kleed me om**, but after a modal it stays whole at the end: **moet me omkleden**.                                                                            |
| I thought the clothes were cheaper.                                              | Ik dacht dat de kleren goedkoper waren.                                                           | **dacht** is the past of **denken** (irregular). After **dat** the verb **waren** goes to the end. **kleren** and **kleding** both mean "clothes".                                                                 |
| Can I make an appointment for next Monday?                                       | Kan ik een afspraak maken voor volgende maandag?                                                  | Question = verb first: **Kan ik ...?** Days of the week are not capitalised in Dutch, and "next Monday" needs no article: **volgende maandag**.                                                                    |
| It’s going to be a great vacation.                                               | Het wordt een geweldige vakantie.                                                                 | **worden** ("to become") is the natural way to say "is going to be". Adjective before a **de**-word with **een** takes **-e**: **een geweldige vakantie**.                                                         |
| The temperature is very hot today.                                               | Het is vandaag erg warm.                                                                          | Dutch describes the weather with **het is ...**, not "the temperature is warm" (you would say **de temperatuur is hoog**). Time (**vandaag**) usually comes before the adjective.                                  |
| The temperature is very cold today.                                              | Het is vandaag erg koud.                                                                          | Same pattern: **het is** + adjective. **erg** and **heel** both mean "very" here.                                                                                                                                  |
| Today is very windy.                                                             | Het is vandaag erg winderig.                                                                      | Again **het is** for weather; Dutch would not make **vandaag** the subject. Also common: **Het waait hard vandaag**.                                                                                               |
| Watch out for the snow.                                                          | Pas op voor de sneeuw.                                                                            | **oppassen** is separable: in a command the prefix goes to the end, **Pas ... op**, and **voor** introduces what to watch out for.                                                                                 |
| There may be hurricanes in the city today.                                       | Er kunnen vandaag orkanen in de stad zijn.                                                        | **er** + verb = "there is/are". Here the modal **kunnen** takes second place and **zijn** goes to the end.                                                                                                         |
| Are there any festivals in town?                                                 | Zijn er festivals in de stad?                                                                     | Question inversion: **Zijn er ...?** = "Are there ...?". "Any" is simply left out.                                                                                                                                 |
| You’re looking good, did you change your hair?                                   | Je ziet er goed uit, heb je je haar veranderd?                                                    | **er goed uitzien** = "to look good": **je ziet er goed uit**, with the separable **uit** at the end. **heb je ... veranderd**: the participle goes to the end.                                                    |
| It seems like it’s going to rain today, where can I buy an umbrella?             | Het lijkt erop dat het vandaag gaat regenen, waar kan ik een paraplu kopen?                       | **Het lijkt erop dat ...** = "It looks like ...". **gaan** + infinitive expresses the near future, like English "going to".                                                                                        |
| It seems that today we will have high temperatures, where can I buy a sunscreen? | Het lijkt erop dat we vandaag hoge temperaturen zullen hebben, waar kan ik zonnebrandcrème kopen? | **zonnebrandcrème** is sunscreen (a **zonnescherm** is an awning). **zullen** marks the future; after **dat** it goes to the end.                                                                                  |
| Am I pronouncing correctly?                                                      | Spreek ik het goed uit?                                                                           | **uitspreken** is separable and needs an object: **Spreek ik het ... uit?** The prefix **uit** goes to the end.                                                                                                    |
| How much does it cost per day?                                                   | Hoeveel kost het per dag?                                                                         | No "does" in Dutch questions: the main verb itself moves before the subject (**kost het**).                                                                                                                        |
| Can you repeat, please?                                                          | Kunt u dat herhalen, alstublieft?                                                                 | **u** + **alstublieft** is the polite form; with friends say **Kun je dat herhalen, alsjeblieft?** Dutch needs an object: **dat**.                                                                                 |
| He was very efficient, so he was promoted.                                       | Hij was erg efficiënt, dus hij kreeg promotie.                                                    | "Promoted" at work is **promotie krijgen** (false friend: **promoveren** means getting a PhD). After **dus** both **dus hij kreeg** and **dus kreeg hij** are correct.                                             |
| I had fish for dinner.                                                           | Ik at vis als avondeten.                                                                          | Dutch doesn't "have" a meal; it eats it: **eten** → past **at**. **als avondeten** = "as dinner".                                                                                                                  |
| I do not like beef.                                                              | Ik hou niet van rundvlees.                                                                        | **houden van** = "to like/love" (spoken form **ik hou**, written also **ik houd**). **rund** + **vlees** = "cattle meat".                                                                                          |
| I came to this city a long time ago.                                             | Ik kwam lang geleden naar deze stad.                                                              | **geleden** = "ago", placed after the time: **lang geleden**. **deze** is used with **de**-words (**de stad**).                                                                                                    |
| Get my shirt, please.                                                            | Haal mijn shirt even, alsjeblieft.                                                                | **halen** = "to go and get/fetch". **even** ("just, for a moment") makes a request friendlier.                                                                                                                     |
| Are you waiting for someone?                                                     | Wacht je op iemand?                                                                               | **wachten op** = "to wait for". In a question with **je** the verb loses its **-t**: **jij wacht** but **wacht je?**                                                                                               |
| Tomorrow is my last day in the city.                                             | Morgen is mijn laatste dag in de stad.                                                            | V2: when **Morgen** comes first, the verb **is** still takes second place.                                                                                                                                         |
| I’m looking for adventures.                                                      | Ik ben op zoek naar avonturen.                                                                    | **op zoek zijn naar** = "to be looking for", a very common alternative to **zoeken**.                                                                                                                              |
| I’m looking to get to know the local culture.                                    | Ik wil de lokale cultuur leren kennen.                                                            | **leren kennen** = "to get to know" (a person or a place). With **wil** both infinitives go to the end.                                                                                                            |
| I like all kinds of music.                                                       | Ik hou van allerlei soorten muziek.                                                               | **allerlei** = "all kinds of"; it never changes form.                                                                                                                                                              |
| I like to meet people on the road.                                               | Ik ontmoet graag mensen onderweg.                                                                 | Verb + **graag** is the standard way to say "I like doing": **ik ontmoet graag** = "I like meeting". **onderweg** = "on the way, while travelling".                                                                |
| I like action movies, but I also like comedy.                                    | Ik hou van actiefilms, maar ik hou ook van komedie.                                               | **maar** ("but") does not change word order. Dutch writes compounds as one word: **actiefilms**.                                                                                                                   |
| I do not like driving at night, I think it’s very dangerous.                     | Ik hou er niet van om ‘s nachts te rijden, ik denk dat het erg gevaarlijk is.                     | **er ... van** points forward to the **om ... te** clause: "I don't like it, to drive at night". **’s nachts** = "at night".                                                                                       |
| I do not like going to the doctor, I’m afraid of vaccines.                       | Ik ga niet graag naar de dokter, ik ben bang voor vaccins.                                        | **niet graag** + verb = "don't like doing". **bang voor** = "afraid of".                                                                                                                                           |
| I do not much like heat, I prefer colder days.                                   | Ik hou niet erg van warmte, ik geef de voorkeur aan koudere dagen.                                | **de voorkeur geven aan** = "to give preference to", a formal "prefer"; in speech **ik heb liever koudere dagen** is more common.                                                                                  |
| I need to drink more water.                                                      | Ik moet meer water drinken.                                                                       | **moeten** = "must/need to"; the infinitive **drinken** goes to the end.                                                                                                                                           |
| I’m fascinated by technology.                                                    | Ik ben gefascineerd door technologie.                                                             | **door** = "by" with a passive or participle. Participles start with **ge-**: **gefascineerd**.                                                                                                                    |
| I need to buy a new computer, my laptop is getting old.                          | Ik moet een nieuwe computer kopen, mijn laptop wordt oud.                                         | **worden** = "to become, get": **wordt oud** = "is getting old".                                                                                                                                                   |
| This is a very complicated situation.                                            | Dit is een zeer gecompliceerde situatie.                                                          | **zeer** is a more formal "very" than **erg**/**heel**. Adjective ending **-e** before a **de**-word: **gecompliceerde situatie**.                                                                                 |
| On the next vacation I go for a cruise.                                          | Tijdens mijn volgende vakantie ga ik op cruise.                                                   | **op cruise gaan** = "to go on a cruise" (like **op vakantie gaan**). Fronted time phrase, so V2 inversion: **ga ik**.                                                                                             |
| One day I want to go back to Disney.                                             | Op een dag wil ik teruggaan naar Disney.                                                          | **Op een dag** = "one day". **teruggaan** is separable but stays whole after the modal **wil**.                                                                                                                    |
| I love pizza, it’s a shame they make you fat.                                    | Ik hou van pizza, het is jammer dat je er dik van wordt.                                          | **ergens dik van worden** = "to get fat from something"; **er ... van** stands for "from it". **Het is jammer dat** = "it's a pity that".                                                                          |
| I need to stop eating so much candy.                                             | Ik moet stoppen met zoveel snoep eten.                                                            | **stoppen met** + plain infinitive (no **te**): **stoppen met roken** = "to stop smoking". **snoep** is uncountable, like "candy".                                                                                 |
| I like to read books from the beginning to the end.                              | Ik hou van boeken lezen van het begin tot het einde.                                              | **van ... tot ...** = "from ... to ...". The shorter **van begin tot eind** is also very common.                                                                                                                   |
| I no longer have the patience to watch television.                               | Ik heb niet langer het geduld om televisie te kijken.                                             | **om ... te** + infinitive = "to (do something)" after a noun like **het geduld**. You **kijken** TV (not **zien**).                                                                                               |
| Fish is a very good food for health.                                             | Vis is een heel goed voedsel voor de gezondheid.                                                  | **voedsel** is a **het**-word, so after **een** the adjective has no **-e**: **een goed voedsel**.                                                                                                                 |
| Life is full of surprises.                                                       | Het leven zit vol verrassingen.                                                                   | Idiom: **vol zitten** = "to be full of", literally "sits full". **het leven** takes the article, unlike English "life".                                                                                            |
| I’m afraid to travel by plane.                                                   | Ik ben bang om met het vliegtuig te reizen.                                                       | Means of transport use **met**: **met het vliegtuig**, **met de trein**. **om ... te** after **bang**.                                                                                                             |
| My friend recommended a great book.                                              | Mijn vriend heeft een geweldig boek aanbevolen.                                                   | Perfect tense with **hebben**; the participle **aanbevolen** (from separable **aanbevelen**) goes to the end. **boek** is a **het**-word: **een geweldig boek**, no **-e**.                                        |
| We are reaching an irreversible point.                                           | We bereiken een onomkeerbaar punt.                                                                | **on-** = "un-/ir-", **-baar** = "-able": **on·omkeer·baar**. **punt** is a **het**-word, so no **-e** after **een**.                                                                                              |
| This is a critical time for world politics.                                      | Dit is een kritieke tijd voor de wereldpolitiek.                                                  | Use **dit is** for "this is" whatever the gender of the noun. **kritiek** + **-e** → **kritieke**.                                                                                                                 |
| The Olympic games are fantastic.                                                 | De Olympische Spelen zijn fantastisch.                                                            | The event name is capitalised: **de Olympische Spelen**. **spel** → plural **spelen**.                                                                                                                             |
| Writing is a task that requires concentration.                                   | Schrijven is een taak die concentratie vereist.                                                   | An infinitive can be a subject (**Schrijven** = "writing"). **die** ("that") refers back to a **de**-word and sends the verb to the end.                                                                           |
| To master a language requires a lot of practice.                                 | Om een taal onder de knie te krijgen, moet je veel oefenen.                                       | Idiom: **iets onder de knie krijgen** = "to master something", literally "get it under the knee". After the fronted **om** clause, V2 gives **moet je**.                                                           |
| Depression is the disease of the century.                                        | Depressie is de ziekte van de eeuw.                                                               | **de eeuw** = "the century". Abstract nouns like **depressie** often need no article.                                                                                                                              |
| Technology changes every day.                                                    | Technologie verandert elke dag.                                                                   | **elke** (or **iedere**) = "every" before **de**-words; before **het**-words use **elk**.                                                                                                                          |
| You need to make wise decisions to achieve your dreams.                          | Je moet verstandige beslissingen nemen om je dromen te verwezenlijken.                            | You "take" decisions in Dutch: **een beslissing nemen**. **om ... te** = "in order to".                                                                                                                            |
| Acquiring vocabulary is only a matter of time and study.                         | Woordenschat verwerven is slechts een kwestie van tijd en studie.                                 | **slechts** is a formal "only" (everyday: **maar** or **alleen**). **een kwestie van** = "a matter of".                                                                                                            |
| Technology can be your biggest ally.                                             | Technologie kan je grootste bondgenoot zijn.                                                      | Modal **kan** second, **zijn** at the end. **grootste** = "biggest" (superlative **-ste**).                                                                                                                        |
| Studying at night can be more productive.                                        | ’s Nachts studeren kan productiever zijn.                                                         | **’s nachts** is an old genitive ("of the night"). At the start of a sentence the next word is capitalised: **’s Nachts**.                                                                                         |
| I want to travel the world and meet new people.                                  | Ik wil de wereld rondreizen en nieuwe mensen ontmoeten.                                           | **rondreizen** = "to travel around". Both infinitives go to the end of their part of the sentence.                                                                                                                 |
| Only fools give up on their dreams.                                              | Alleen dwazen geven hun dromen op.                                                                | **opgeven** is separable: **geven ... op**, with the prefix at the end.                                                                                                                                            |
| Our planet is polluted.                                                          | Onze planeet is vervuild.                                                                         | **onze** is used with **de**-words and plurals; with **het**-words it is **ons** (**ons huis**).                                                                                                                   |
| I do not like to do the dishes, but it’s necessary.                              | Ik hou niet van de afwas, maar het is noodzakelijk.                                               | **de afwas** = "the dishes"; **afwassen** = "to do the dishes".                                                                                                                                                    |
| I consider myself an organized person.                                           | Ik beschouw mezelf als een georganiseerd persoon.                                                 | **beschouwen als** = "to regard as". With **persoon** the adjective often drops its **-e** (**een aardig persoon**); **georganiseerde persoon** is also correct.                                                   |
| I like to acquire new knowledge.                                                 | Ik vind het leuk om nieuwe kennis op te doen.                                                     | **Ik vind het leuk om ... te** = "I enjoy ...". With a separable verb **te** goes in between: **op te doen**.                                                                                                      |
| History is a fascinating subject.                                                | De geschiedenis is een fascinerend onderwerp.                                                     | **onderwerp** is a **het**-word, so no **-e** after **een**: **een fascinerend onderwerp**.                                                                                                                        |
| Mathematics and physics are not as difficult as they seem.                       | Wiskunde en natuurkunde zijn niet zo moeilijk als ze lijken.                                      | **niet zo ... als** = "not as ... as". **lijken** = "to seem" (also "to resemble").                                                                                                                                |
| You have to fight for your dreams.                                               | Je moet vechten voor je dromen.                                                                   | **je** here is the general "you" (= one). The infinitive may come before a prepositional phrase: **vechten voor je dromen**.                                                                                       |
| You need to control your expectations.                                           | Je moet je verwachtingen beheersen.                                                               | The first **je** is "you", the second is "your". **beheersen** = "to control, master".                                                                                                                             |
| The electric cars are fantastic.                                                 | De elektrische auto’s zijn fantastisch.                                                           | Nouns ending in a vowel like **auto** take **’s** in the plural: **auto’s**. Adjective before a plural gets **-e**: **elektrische**.                                                                               |
| I love technology.                                                               | Ik hou van technologie.                                                                           | **houden van** covers both "like" and "love".                                                                                                                                                                      |
| I like light clothes.                                                            | Ik hou van lichte kleding.                                                                        | **licht** means both "light (weight)" and "light (colour)". **kleding** is a singular collective noun.                                                                                                             |
| I love animals.                                                                  | Ik hou van dieren.                                                                                | **dier** → plural **dieren** (the common **-en** plural).                                                                                                                                                          |
| Volleyball is an interesting sport but I prefer basketball.                      | Volleybal is een interessante sport, maar ik heb liever basketbal.                                | **liever hebben** = "to prefer" (**graag** → **liever** → **liefst**). Dutch puts a comma before **maar**.                                                                                                         |
| I like big TVs.                                                                  | Ik hou van grote tv’s.                                                                            | **tv** is said "tee-vee"; plural **tv’s** with an apostrophe.                                                                                                                                                      |
| I like decoration, especially the modern styles.                                 | Ik hou van decoratie, vooral de moderne stijlen.                                                  | **vooral** = "especially, mainly".                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| I read less books than I would like.                                             | Ik lees minder boeken dan ik zou willen.                                                          | **dan** = "than" in comparisons (not **als**). **minder** works for both "less" and "fewer".                                                                                                                       |
| I like going to the movies every week.                                           | Ik ga graag elke week naar de bioscoop.                                                           | **graag** again for "like doing". **de bioscoop** = "the cinema"; "going to the movies" = **naar de bioscoop gaan**.                                                                                               |
| I like collecting coins.                                                         | Ik vind het leuk om munten te verzamelen.                                                         | **munt** → plural **munten**. **vind het leuk om ... te** is an alternative to verb + **graag**.                                                                                                                   |
| I can’t go alone.                                                                | Ik kan niet alleen gaan.                                                                          | **alleen** = "alone" here, but also "only" (as in **Alleen dwazen ...**).                                                                                                                                          |
| I like to play sports at night.                                                  | Ik hou van sporten in de nacht.                                                                   | **sporten** is one verb meaning "to play sports". **’s avonds** ("in the evening") is often what people actually mean.                                                                                             |
| I prefer to play sports during the day.                                          | Ik geef er de voorkeur aan om overdag te sporten.                                                 | **er ... aan** points forward to the **om ... te** clause. **overdag** = "during the day".                                                                                                                         |
| I prefer long projects.                                                          | Ik geef de voorkeur aan lange projecten.                                                          | **project** is a **het**-word, but in the plural it is always **de** and the adjective gets **-e**: **lange projecten**.                                                                                           |
| I’m looking for new projects.                                                    | Ik ben op zoek naar nieuwe projecten.                                                             | **op zoek naar** again; equivalent to **Ik zoek nieuwe projecten**.                                                                                                                                                |
| A long time ago.                                                                 | Lang geleden.                                                                                     | Dutch simply says "long ago": **lang geleden**, not **een lange tijd geleden**.                                                                                                                                    |
| Are these products the same?                                                     | Zijn deze producten hetzelfde?                                                                    | **hetzelfde** = "the same" (written as one word). **deze** with plurals.                                                                                                                                           |
| Are you afraid?                                                                  | Ben je bang?                                                                                      | **zijn** inverts: **jij bent** but **ben je?** (the **-t** drops before **je**).                                                                                                                                   |
| Will you be busy tonight?                                                        | Heb je het vanavond druk?                                                                         | Idiom: **het druk hebben** = "to be busy" (a person); **druk zijn** usually means "hectic" or "hyperactive". **vanavond** = "tonight/this evening".                                                                |
| Are you coming tonight?                                                          | Kom je vanavond?                                                                                  | Present tense for near-future plans is normal: **Kom je vanavond?**                                                                                                                                                |
| Have a good trip.                                                                | Goede reis.                                                                                       | Set wish: **Goede reis!** Similar: **Prettige vakantie!**, **Fijne dag!**                                                                                                                                          |
| Have you eaten?                                                                  | Heb jij al gegeten?                                                                               | Perfect with **hebben**: **heb ... gegeten**, participle at the end. **al** = "already/yet".                                                                                                                       |
| Have you seen this movie before?                                                 | Heb je deze film eerder gezien?                                                                   | **eerder** = "before, earlier". **zien** → participle **gezien**.                                                                                                                                                  |
| He works on the computer.                                                        | Hij werkt op de computer.                                                                         | **op de computer** = "on the computer". **werken** → **hij werkt** (stem + **-t**).                                                                                                                                |
| We’ll be back in twenty minutes.                                                 | We zijn over twintig minuten terug.                                                               | **over** + time = "in ... (from now)". Dutch uses the present **zijn** for a near future.                                                                                                                          |
| Here is your order.                                                              | Hier is uw bestelling.                                                                            | **uw** is the formal "your", the norm for staff speaking to customers.                                                                                                                                             |
| He was the fastest in the race.                                                  | Hij was de snelste in de race.                                                                    | Superlative **snel** → **snelst**, with **de** + **-e**: **de snelste**. **wedstrijd** is a common alternative to **race**.                                                                                        |
| This is the kitchen, this is the living room and this is the bedroom.            | Dit is de keuken, dit is de woonkamer en dit is de slaapkamer.                                    | Compounds: **woon·kamer** ("living room"), **slaap·kamer** ("sleeping room"). **dit is** works with **de**- and **het**-words.                                                                                     |
| He’s a great student.                                                            | Hij is een geweldige student.                                                                     | **student** is a **de**-word, so **-e**: **een geweldige student**.                                                                                                                                                |
| She’s a wonderful writer.                                                        | Ze is een geweldige schrijver.                                                                    | **schrijver** is often used for women too, but **schrijfster** is the specifically female form.                                                                                                                    |
| The doctor is very competent.                                                    | De dokter is zeer competent.                                                                      | Adjectives after the verb (**is competent**) never take **-e**. **dokter** is everyday; **arts** is more formal.                                                                                                   |
| I’ve never been to France.                                                       | Ik ben nog nooit in Frankrijk geweest.                                                            | Perfect with **zijn**: **ben ... geweest**. Dutch says "been in France" (**in**), not "to". **nog nooit** = "never (yet)".                                                                                         |
| She is very famous.                                                              | Ze is erg beroemd.                                                                                | **Erg** and **heel** both mean "very" in everyday speech; **zeer** is more formal. **Ze** is the unstressed form of **zij**.                                                                                       |
| We are working hard on the project.                                              | We werken hard aan het project.                                                                   | Dutch has no "-ing" form: the simple present **we werken** covers "we work" and "we are working". You work **aan** (on) something; **project** is a het-word.                                                      |
| Our family is very close.                                                        | Ons gezin is heel hecht.                                                                          | **Hecht** means close-knit; **dichtbij** only means physically near. **Gezin** (the household family) is a het-word, so "our" is **ons**, not **onze**.                                                            |
| I like watching television at night.                                             | Ik hou ervan om ‘s nachts televisie te kijken.                                                    | **Houden van** = to love/like; with a following action it becomes **ervan om ... te** + infinitive, which goes at the end. **'s nachts** is "at night" (from old "des nachts"); for the evening say **'s avonds**. |
| I lost my watch.                                                                 | Ik ben mijn horloge kwijt.                                                                        | **Kwijt zijn** (to have lost, to be without) is the everyday way to say you can't find something. **Horloge** is a het-word, and the g sounds like the French "j".                                                 |
| I made a serious mistake.                                                        | Ik heb een ernstige fout gemaakt.                                                                 | Perfect tense: **hebben** + past participle, and the participle (**gemaakt**) goes to the end. **Ernstig** gets **-e** before the noun: **een ernstige fout**.                                                     |
| I made this cake.                                                                | Ik heb deze cake gemaakt.                                                                         | **Cake** is a de-word, so "this" is **deze** (het-words use **dit**). In the Netherlands **cake** is a loaf cake; a decorated cake is a **taart**.                                                                 |
| I need a new house key.                                                          | Ik heb een nieuwe huissleutel nodig.                                                              | "To need" is **nodig hebben**: **hebben** is conjugated and **nodig** goes to the end. **Huis + sleutel** joins into one compound word.                                                                            |
| I speak three languages.                                                         | Ik spreek drie talen.                                                                             | Plural of **taal** is **talen**: the double vowel becomes single in an open syllable but keeps its long sound.                                                                                                     |
| I remember this situation.                                                       | Ik herinner me deze situatie.                                                                     | **Zich herinneren** (to remember) is reflexive: you "remind yourself", hence **me**. Nouns in **-tie** are de-words, so **deze situatie**.                                                                         |
| Remind me to call her.                                                           | Herinner me eraan haar te bellen.                                                                 | **Iemand herinneren aan iets** = to remind someone of something; **eraan** points ahead to the **te** + infinitive part. Imperative = the bare verb stem: **herinner**.                                            |
| Let’s take a look.                                                               | Laten we kijken.                                                                                  | **Laten we** + infinitive is the standard way to say "let's". Adding **even** (**Laten we even kijken**) makes it sound casual.                                                                                    |
| Let’s share this news.                                                           | Laten we dit nieuws delen.                                                                        | **Laten we** again, with the infinitive at the end. **Nieuws** is a het-word and grammatically singular, so **dit nieuws**.                                                                                        |
| My birthday is April 29th.                                                       | Mijn verjaardag is 29 april.                                                                      | Dutch dates: day before month, plain number (no "th"), and months are not capitalised.                                                                                                                             |
| My daughter is here.                                                             | Mijn dochter is hier.                                                                             | Literally: "My daughter is here." The **ch** in **dochter** is the guttural Dutch g-sound.                                                                                                                         |
| My father is a lawyer.                                                           | Mijn vader is advocaat.                                                                           | Dutch drops the article before professions after **zijn** or **worden**: **hij is leraar**, **ze is arts**. **Advocaat** is a false friend: it means lawyer (and also an egg liqueur).                             |
| My son studies engineering.                                                      | Mijn zoon studeert techniek.                                                                      | You **studeren** a subject directly, with no preposition. **Techniek** is the general word for engineering; an engineer is an **ingenieur**.                                                                       |
| No thank you very much.                                                          | Nee, heel erg bedankt.                                                                            | **Heel erg** stacks two intensifiers ("really very") and is common in speech. **Bedankt** is a neutral "thanks"; **dank u wel** is the formal version.                                                             |
| No one is helping to solve the problem.                                          | Niemand helpt het probleem op te lossen.                                                          | **Oplossen** is a separable verb, so in the **te** form the **te** goes inside it: **op te lossen**. **Niemand** takes a singular verb (**helpt**).                                                                |
| Do you want to go now or later?                                                  | Wil je nu of later gaan?                                                                          | Questions invert subject and verb: **wil je**. With a modal like **willen**, the infinitive (**gaan**) goes to the very end. When **je** follows the verb, the **-t** drops: **wil je**, not "wilt je".            |
| Take this medicine.                                                              | Neem dit medicijn in.                                                                             | **Innemen** (to take medicine) is separable: in the imperative the prefix **in** goes to the end. The plain infinitive form (**innemen**) is what you see on medicine labels.                                      |
| I miss you.                                                                      | Ik mis je.                                                                                        | Same structure as English. **Missen** also covers missing a bus or a chance: **Ik heb de bus gemist**.                                                                                                             |
| This restaurant is not expensive.                                                | Dit restaurant is niet duur.                                                                      | Use **niet** to negate adjectives, verbs and definite things; **geen** is for nouns with "a" or no article. **Restaurant** is a het-word, so **dit**.                                                              |
| This is a great school.                                                          | Dit is een geweldige school.                                                                      | **Dit is** introduces anything, even de-words like **school**. **Geweldig** takes **-e** before the noun after **een** with a de-word.                                                                             |
| The book is under the table.                                                     | Het boek staat onder de tafel.                                                                    | Dutch often uses position verbs instead of "is": **staan** (upright), **liggen** (lying flat), **zitten** (inside or stuck). An upright book **staat**.                                                            |
| The book is near the table.                                                      | Het boek staat bij de tafel.                                                                      | **Bij** = near, at, beside (also "at someone's place": **bij mij**). Position verb **staan** again.                                                                                                                |
| The book is on the table.                                                        | Het boek ligt op tafel.                                                                           | A book lying flat **ligt**. **Op tafel** without **de** is a fixed expression, like "on the table" for set places.                                                                                                 |
| The book is behind the table.                                                    | Het boek staat achter de tafel.                                                                   | **Achter** = behind; its opposite is **voor** (in front of). The **ch** is guttural.                                                                                                                               |
| What do they study?                                                              | Wat studeren ze?                                                                                  | No "do" helper in Dutch questions: the main verb itself moves before the subject (**studeren ze**).                                                                                                                |
| What do you recommend?                                                           | Wat raden jullie aan?                                                                             | **Aanraden** (to recommend) is separable, so **aan** goes to the end. **Jullie** is plural "you", for asking the staff in general; to one person say **Wat raadt u aan?**                                          |
| What do you want to do tonight?                                                  | Wat wil je vanavond doen?                                                                         | Modal + infinitive: **wil** is second, **doen** goes last, and the time word sits in between. **Vanavond** = this evening, tonight.                                                                                |
| What is the meaning of this word?                                                | Wat is de betekenis van dit woord?                                                                | **Woord** is a het-word, so **dit woord**. A shorter alternative: **Wat betekent dit woord?**                                                                                                                      |
| What is your favorite food?                                                      | Wat is je favoriete eten?                                                                         | Correct, but Dutch speakers often use the compound **lievelingseten** (**lieveling** = darling, favourite) instead of **favoriete**.                                                                               |
| What’s your last name?                                                           | Wat is je achternaam?                                                                             | **Achternaam** is literally "behind name"; your first name is your **voornaam**. Formal: **Wat is uw achternaam?**                                                                                                 |
| This amount is enough.                                                           | Dit bedrag is voldoende.                                                                          | **Bedrag** (sum of money) is a het-word. **Voldoende** is a bit formal; in speech you'd also hear **genoeg**.                                                                                                      |
| I borrowed this book from my friend.                                             | Ik heb dit boek geleend van mijn vriend.                                                          | **Lenen** means both "to borrow" and "to lend"; **van** (from) tells you it's borrowing. More often the participle goes fully last: **...van mijn vriend geleend**.                                                |
| This car is very expensive.                                                      | Deze auto is erg duur.                                                                            | **Auto** is a de-word, so **deze**. **Duur** means expensive (and also "duration" as a noun).                                                                                                                      |
| That sucks for our future.                                                       | Dat is slecht voor onze toekomst.                                                                 | A softer rendering of "that sucks" (literally "that's bad"). **Toekomst** is a de-word, so "our" is **onze**.                                                                                                      |
| This afternoon too, we must return.                                              | Ook vanmiddag moeten we terugkeren.                                                               | Inversion: when something other than the subject comes first (**ook vanmiddag**), the verb stays second and the subject follows: **moeten we**. **Terugkeren** is separable.                                       |
| This is completely wrong.                                                        | Dit is helemaal verkeerd.                                                                         | **Helemaal** = completely; **helemaal niet** = not at all. **Verkeerd** means wrong in the sense of incorrect.                                                                                                     |
| The car is in the garage.                                                        | De auto is in de garage.                                                                          | Fine as is, but Dutch usually says the car **staat** in the garage (position verb). **Garage** keeps its French pronunciation.                                                                                     |
| We need to fix the car.                                                          | We moeten de auto repareren.                                                                      | **Moeten** + infinitive at the end expresses need or obligation. Many verbs in **-eren** (**repareren**) come from French and are regular.                                                                         |
| The roads are slippery.                                                          | De wegen zijn glad.                                                                               | **Glad** means slippery (icy roads) and also smooth. Plural **weg** → **wegen**.                                                                                                                                   |
| There are some apples in the refrigerator.                                       | Er zijn appels in de koelkast.                                                                    | **Er zijn** = there are; **er** fills the subject slot. Dutch often uses a position verb here too: **Er liggen appels in de koelkast**.                                                                            |
| They arrived yesterday.                                                          | Ze zijn gisteren aangekomen.                                                                      | Verbs of movement towards a destination take **zijn** in the perfect, not **hebben**. **Aankomen** is separable, so **ge** goes inside: **aangekomen**.                                                            |
| They still do not know each other.                                               | Ze kennen elkaar nog steeds niet.                                                                 | **Elkaar** = each other. **Niet** comes after the object here, near the end. **Kennen** is knowing a person or place; **weten** is knowing a fact.                                                                 |
| This house is very large.                                                        | Dit huis is erg groot.                                                                            | **Huis** is a het-word: **dit huis**, **het huis**. Plural **huizen** (s becomes z).                                                                                                                               |
| This game is very difficult.                                                     | Deze game is erg moeilijk.                                                                        | The English loanword **game** is a de-word; the native word **spel** is a het-word (**dit spel**).                                                                                                                 |
| This game is very time consuming.                                                | Deze game kost veel tijd.                                                                         | Literally "costs a lot of time": Dutch prefers this phrase over a single adjective (**tijdrovend** exists but is formal).                                                                                          |
| I want a glass of water, please.                                                 | Ik wil een glas water, alsjeblieft.                                                               | No word for "of" in quantities: **een glas water**, **een kop koffie**. **Ik wil** sounds blunt; **ik wil graag** is politer.                                                                                      |
| Did you have a problem along the way?                                            | Heb je onderweg een probleem gehad?                                                               | Perfect tense in a question: **heb** comes first, the participle **gehad** goes last. **Onderweg** = on the way.                                                                                                   |
| What are your hobbies?                                                           | Wat zijn je hobby's?                                                                              | Words ending in a vowel such as **-y** form the plural with an apostrophe + s: **hobby's**, **baby's**.                                                                                                            |
| What do you think about this?                                                    | Wat denk je hiervan?                                                                              | **Hiervan** = "of this" (**hier** + **van**); Dutch fuses "this/that" with prepositions like this. For opinions **Wat vind je hiervan?** is even more common.                                                      |
| This is a very small town.                                                       | Dit is een heel klein stadje.                                                                     | Diminutive: **stad** + **-je** = **stadje**, a small town. All diminutives are het-words, and after **een** with a het-word the adjective takes no **-e**: **klein**.                                              |
| When do you start working?                                                       | Wanneer begin je te werken?                                                                       | After a question word, the verb comes next and then the subject: **Wanneer begin je**. **Beginnen** is followed by **te** + infinitive.                                                                            |
| I need to go to the market.                                                      | Ik moet naar de markt gaan.                                                                       | **Naar** = to (a destination). In speech **gaan** is often dropped after a modal: **Ik moet naar de markt**.                                                                                                       |
| Where are the new shirts?                                                        | Waar zijn de nieuwe shirts?                                                                       | Before a noun with **de**, the adjective always takes **-e**: **de nieuwe shirts**. Loanwords often form the plural with **-s**.                                                                                   |
| Where are you?                                                                   | Waar ben jij?                                                                                     | **Jij** is the stressed form; **Waar ben je?** is the neutral everyday version. Verb drops **-t** after inversion: **ben jij**, not "bent jij".                                                                    |
| Where can we rent cheap cars?                                                    | Waar kunnen we goedkope auto’s huren?                                                             | Modal **kunnen** second, **huren** at the end. **Auto's** takes an apostrophe because it ends in a long vowel. **Goedkoop** literally means "good buy".                                                            |
| Where did you go in the morning?                                                 | Waar ging je vanochtend naartoe?                                                                  | **Vanochtend** (or **vanmorgen**) = this morning; **morgen** alone means tomorrow. **Waar ... naartoe** splits "where to" around the clause.                                                                       |
| Where did you learn this language?                                               | Waar heb je deze taal geleerd?                                                                    | Dutch often uses the perfect for completed past actions where English uses the simple past. **Leren** means both "to learn" and "to teach".                                                                        |
| Where did you work before?                                                       | Waar werkte je eerder?                                                                            | Simple past of regular verbs: stem + **-te** or **-de** (**werkte**). For "previously, in your last job" you'll also hear **Waar werkte je hiervoor?**                                                             |
| Where’s the mailbox on this avenue?                                              | Waar is de brievenbus op deze weg?                                                                | **Brievenbus** is both the public postbox and the letterbox in your door. You live **in** a street: **in deze straat**; **laan** is the word for avenue.                                                           |
| Who is this person?                                                              | Wie is deze persoon?                                                                              | **Wie** = who, **wat** = what. **Persoon** is a de-word, so **deze**.                                                                                                                                              |
| Did you send me the letter?                                                      | Heb je me de brief gestuurd?                                                                      | Indirect object (**me**) before direct object (**de brief**), as in English. Yes/no questions simply start with the verb.                                                                                          |
| Who was your teacher?                                                            | Wie was je leraar?                                                                                | **Leraar** (male or general) / **lerares** (female); in primary school children say **juf** and **meester**.                                                                                                       |
| Why are you laughing?                                                            | Waarom lach je?                                                                                   | Simple present covers "are laughing" (no "-ing" form). **Lach je**: the **-t** drops when **je** follows the verb.                                                                                                 |
| Can you pass me the salt please?                                                 | Kun je me alsjeblieft het zout geven?                                                             | **Kun je** is the most common polite request form. At the table **aangeven** (to pass) is the exact verb: **Kun je me het zout aangeven?** **Zout** is a het-word.                                                 |
| Can you take me home?                                                            | Kun je me naar huis brengen?                                                                      | **Naar huis** = (to) home, as a destination; **thuis** = at home. **Brengen** = to take or bring someone somewhere.                                                                                                |
| Do you prefer soda or water?                                                     | Heeft u liever frisdrank of water?                                                                | "To prefer" = **liever hebben** (literally "have rather"). This is the formal **u** form; **u** takes **heeft**. Informal: **Heb je liever...?**                                                                   |
| I’m hungry, I need to eat.                                                       | Ik heb honger, ik moet eten.                                                                      | Dutch "has" hunger: **honger hebben**, just like **dorst hebben** (to be thirsty).                                                                                                                                 |
| What do you like watching on television?                                         | Wat vind je leuk om op televisie te kijken?                                                       | **Leuk vinden** = to like or enjoy. The **om ... te** + infinitive part goes at the end of the sentence.                                                                                                           |
| Do you prefer Android or iOS?                                                    | Heb je liever Android of iOS?                                                                     | **Liever hebben** again, informal this time. **Of** means "or" (and also "whether").                                                                                                                               |
| I still have not decided on the color of the wall.                               | Ik heb nog steeds geen beslissing genomen over de kleur van de muur.                              | Literally "taken no decision": **een beslissing nemen**, negated with **geen** because it's a noun with **een**. Simpler: **Ik heb nog niet besloten**.                                                            |
| This looks delicious.                                                            | Dit ziet er heerlijk uit.                                                                         | **Eruitzien** (to look, appear) is separable: **ziet** is second, **er** sits in the middle and **uit** goes to the end.                                                                                           |
| I trust you, do not let me down.                                                 | Ik vertrouw je, laat me niet in de steek.                                                         | **Iemand in de steek laten** = to let someone down, abandon them. **Niet** comes before the fixed expression.                                                                                                      |
| I need to buy a map of the city.                                                 | Ik moet een kaart van de stad kopen.                                                              | **Kaart** is a map (also a card or ticket). A city street map is specifically a **plattegrond**.                                                                                                                   |
| I would like water, please.                                                      | Ik zou graag water willen, alsjeblieft.                                                           | **Ik zou graag ... willen** is the polite "I would like"; shorter: **Ik wil graag water**. **Graag** means "gladly" and softens requests.                                                                          |
| Can I use your internet?                                                         | Kan ik je internet gebruiken?                                                                     | **Kan ik** (can I) vs **mag ik** (may I): both work for permission. **Gebruiken** goes to the end.                                                                                                                 |
| Call me later?                                                                   | Bel me later?                                                                                     | **Opbellen** or simply **bellen** = to phone; the bare stem is the imperative. As a fuller question: **Bel je me later?**                                                                                          |
| Hello, who’s speaking?                                                           | Hallo, met wie spreek ik?                                                                         | Fixed phone phrase: literally "with whom do I speak?". Dutch speakers answer the phone with **met** + their name: **Met Anna**.                                                                                    |
| Hello, may I speak to Mary?                                                      | Hallo, mag ik met Mary praten?                                                                    | **Mag ik** = may I. On the phone **Mag ik Mary spreken?** is the more standard phrasing; **praten** is chatting.                                                                                                   |
| I paid for dinner.                                                               | Ik heb betaald voor het avondeten.                                                                | A prepositional phrase may follow the participle in speech. You can also drop **voor**: **Ik heb het avondeten betaald**.                                                                                          |
| Talk to you soon.                                                                | Spreek je snel.                                                                                   | A casual sign-off, short for **Ik spreek je snel**. Also common: **Spreek je!** or **Tot snel!**                                                                                                                   |
| I’m 30 years old.                                                                | Ik ben 30 jaar oud.                                                                               | **Jaar** stays singular after a number: **30 jaar**, not "jaren". **Oud** can be dropped: **Ik ben 30**.                                                                                                           |
| How old are you?                                                                 | Hoe oud ben je?                                                                                   | **Hoe** + adjective asks "how": **hoe oud**, **hoe groot**. Formal: **Hoe oud bent u?**                                                                                                                            |
| I’m a beginner.                                                                  | Ik ben een beginner.                                                                              | Unlike with professions (**ik ben leraar**), **een** is normal here because **beginner** describes a level rather than a job.                                                                                      |
| I’ll be home in 4 hours.                                                         | Ik ben over vier uur thuis.                                                                       | The present tense often replaces the future when a time is given. **Over** + time = in (from now); **uur** stays singular after a number.                                                                          |
| I’m happy, and you?                                                              | Ik ben blij, en jij?                                                                              | **Jij** is stressed because it contrasts with **ik**. **Blij** is happy or glad; **gelukkig** is deeper happiness.                                                                                                 |
| I’ll be leaving town tomorrow.                                                   | Ik ga morgen de stad uit.                                                                         | **Gaan** + present tense expresses the near future. **De stad uit** = out of town: **uit** after the noun shows movement out.                                                                                      |
| I’m not married.                                                                 | Ik ben niet getrouwd.                                                                             | **Getrouwd** works as an adjective here, negated with **niet**. To get married = **trouwen**.                                                                                                                      |
| I’m waiting for your call.                                                       | Ik wacht op je telefoontje.                                                                       | You wait **op** (on) something, not "for". **Telefoontje** is a diminutive of **telefoon** and means a phone call.                                                                                                 |
| I’m sorry, but we’re exhausted.                                                  | Het spijt me, maar we zijn uitgeput.                                                              | **Het spijt me** = literally "it grieves me", the standard apology; **sorry** is also very common. **Uitgeput** = exhausted.                                                                                       |
| She is a computer expert.                                                        | Ze is een computerexpert.                                                                         | Dutch writes compounds as one word: **computer + expert** = **computerexpert**.                                                                                                                                    |
| She is beautiful and elegant.                                                    | Ze is mooi en elegant.                                                                            | No **-e** on adjectives after **is** (predicative position): **ze is mooi**, but **een mooie vrouw**.                                                                                                              |
| Sometimes I go to bed too late.                                                  | Soms ga ik te laat naar bed.                                                                      | **Soms** comes first, so verb and subject swap: **ga ik**. **Naar bed gaan** = to go to bed.                                                                                                                       |
| Sorry, I do not have a pen.                                                      | Sorry, ik heb geen pen.                                                                           | **Geen** replaces "not a/any" before nouns: **geen pen**, not "niet een pen".                                                                                                                                      |
| Turn the car on, let’s go to the next town.                                      | Zet de auto aan, laten we naar de volgende stad gaan.                                             | **Aanzetten** (to switch on) is separable: **zet ... aan**. For a car you'll also hear **Start de auto**.                                                                                                          |
| Stop the car, I need to go to the pharmacy.                                      | Stop de auto, ik moet naar de apotheek gaan.                                                      | **Apotheek** is a pharmacy (prescriptions); a **drogist** sells everyday toiletries and simple medicine.                                                                                                           |
| Seriously? I did not expect that.                                                | Serieus? Dat had ik niet verwacht.                                                                | **Serieus?** or **Echt?** express surprise; **ernstig** means serious as in grave. **Dat** comes first, so the verb follows it: **had ik**.                                                                        |
| I do not like to wait.                                                           | Ik hou niet van wachten.                                                                          | **Houden van** + infinitive as a noun (**wachten** = waiting). **Hou** is the spoken form of **houd**.                                                                                                             |
| No one is helping.                                                               | Niemand helpt.                                                                                    | Literally: "Nobody helps." Present simple again for an ongoing action.                                                                                                                                             |
| This square is under construction.                                               | Dit plein is in aanbouw.                                                                          | **In aanbouw** = being built (**bouwen** = to build). **Plein** is a het-word, so **dit plein**.                                                                                                                   |
